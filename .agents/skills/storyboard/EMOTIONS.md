# Emotions and hooks

Use one main emotion in each video. Add one support emotion if necessary.

## Placement

- Show the hook in the first 3 s: a question or a strange fact.
- Put the turn at 60 to 70 % of the time.
- End on the answer. Do not end on the threat.

## Emotions

| Emotion | Use | Pixel art | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | Open a gap that this video closes. | A "?" box in the HUD unlocks at the answer. | A gap that stays open is bait. |
| Surprise | A fact that breaks a common belief. | The character freezes; a "!" appears above its head. | An overstated fact destroys trust. |
| Suspense (test) | An experiment with an unknown result. | A progress bar fills while the mouse waits. | False stakes if you hide a known result. |
| Relief, "aha" | At the turn, after tension. | The HUD shows "LEVEL CLEAR"; the cells light up. | Relief too early removes the tension. |
| Awe | A large scale of size or time. | A zoom out from one cell to the body or the Earth. | A slow zoom loses the viewer. |
| Character humour | Make a hard idea friendly. | A T cell with a small sword shrugs. | The joke hides the fact. |
| Moderate fear, anxiety | A real threat with a clear mechanism. | A red HUD alarm; a health bar falls. | A threat without an answer makes viewers turn away. |
| Empathy | Give a cell or a scientist a goal. | A face that changes at each beat. | Human motives can suggest a wrong mechanism. |

## Rules for honest use

1. Pair each threat with an answer in the same video. Show what science does.
2. Keep the threat in proportion. Give a real number and a source for each risk.
3. Do not make false stakes. If the result is known, say it.
4. Do not give health advice. Do not tell viewers what to take or do.
5. Aim fear at the disease, not at the patient. Do not make patients the joke.
6. Say "in mice" for mouse results. Do not promise a cure.
